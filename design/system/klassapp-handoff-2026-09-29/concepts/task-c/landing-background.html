Landing background, hero included

This audit was read from landing-preview.css (the staging copy you uploaded). The renders are structural skeletons that use the real background CSS, with grey blocks standing in for content, so compare them for the backgrounds only. They were rendered in this browser, not on staging.

What's there now, top to bottom

  • Hero: .hero-bg-vintage is a paper radial (base → mid → deep), an ink-wash vignette, edge browning, and 32px ruled lines. It stops hard at the hero's bottom edge, so there's a visible seam into the flat paper below.
  • Body: flat --paper-base #FAFAF5 for everything else. Depth comes only from per-section radial glows: violet in how-it-works (0.04), green in trust (0.07), blue, violet and amber in the mesh, and violet or amber on each Toshi card. That's five tints with no shared rule.
  • Dot grid: it exists only inside .toshi-visual::after, 1px at 5% ink, masked to fade downward. It's a single-use motif.
  • Hero cards: white device frames with a cool slate shadow on warm paper. They read slightly pasted-on; the debox concept softened their borders but not their shadow hue.
  • Footer: #F8FAFC, a cool slate. It's the only cool surface on the page, so it breaks from the paper at the very end.
  • After debox: the sections no longer have card walls to separate them, so the background has to carry the rhythm. Right now it can't, because it's one flat colour.

Alternate sections sit on --paper-mid bands, each with 64px feathered edges rather than hard seams. After debox, this gives the page scannable rhythm. The trade-off: FAQ and protocol shift if a section is added or removed, so the band order must be set per section, not by :nth-child.

C · Quiet grid

The orbit's dot grid becomes the page's connective tissue: one static, page-wide warm-ink dot layer at 9% on a 24px grid, fading in after the hero and out before the footer. It's the strongest "infrastructure" signal. It's also the busiest option, and on low-contrast phone screens it can read as noise. The orbit panel drops its own grid so it isn't doubled.

Rules for whichever you pick

  • Everything is static: no animated backgrounds, no background-attachment: fixed (it's expensive on mobile), and no blur.
  • Every background uses --paper-* tokens only. No new colours, and text contrast is unchanged because each surface stays at or lighter than --paper-mid (#F3F0E8). --text-secondary #64748B on #F3F0E8 calculates to 4.18:1, which fails 4.5:1. Option A or B therefore requires body copy on mid-paper to use #475569 (6.65:1 calculated). This is not browser-measured.
  • Nothing changes under reduced motion, because nothing moves.

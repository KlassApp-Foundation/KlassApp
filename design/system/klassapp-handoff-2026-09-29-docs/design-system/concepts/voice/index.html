Voice and copy guide

This covers the docs, WhatsApp messages and social posts. The readers are head teachers, bursars, class teachers and parents at schools from nursery to secondary, in many countries. Many read English as a second or third language, often on a small phone with patchy data.

Positioning: say it exactly like this

Tagline

An open education protocol for humans and agents.

Description

KlassApp is an education protocol that runs in the tools educationists already use. Admins manage school operations in Slack, teachers enter marks from spreadsheets, and parents receive their children’s school updates on WhatsApp, all by chatting in natural language with Toshi, your school’s AI assistant.

Use both as written wherever KlassApp is introduced: the landing page, docs home, blog, social bios, pitch title slide and app listings. Shorter contexts take the tagline alone. Don't paraphrase them or stack another slogan next to them.

Retired lines: The school platform that operates in the tools educationists already use, Open-source agentic school protocol, Smarter schools start here, School operations on one protocol. Never position KlassApp by country, region ("East African schools") or one school level; it's for schools from nursery to secondary, everywhere.

Toshi exception: the description calls Toshi "your school's AI assistant". That wording is fixed in the description only. Everywhere else, follow the Toshi rules below.

Five rules

  • Plain English. Short sentences of about 15 words, one idea each. Use common words: "use", not "utilise"; "start", not "commence".
  • Say "you". Talk to one person. In the docs, KlassApp doesn't say "we" except in the help and contact lines. In social posts, "we" is the KlassApp team.
  • Use the school's words. Class, stream, term, report card, fees, class teacher, head teacher, bursar, and the class names, exams and currency the school itself uses (from its settings). Write British spelling: "colour", "enrol", "programme", "licence" (noun).
  • Name things exactly as the app does. Buttons and menu items appear in bold, word for word: Save attendance, not "the save button".
  • Only say what works today. Describe a feature only if a school can use it right now. Anything planned is labelled "Coming" and says "we're building", never "you can". There are no dates for unshipped work.

Toshi

  • Toshi is KlassApp's assistant. Write "Toshi", never "the AI", "Toshi AI" or "the Toshi bot". Use "it", not "he" or "she". (The fixed description's "your school's AI assistant" is the one exception.)
  • On first mention in a document: "Toshi, KlassApp's assistant". After that, just "Toshi".
  • Toshi helps and suggests; staff approve. Don't write that Toshi decides, knows or thinks.
  • Don't name the model vendors behind Toshi in parent-facing copy. On the landing page, the "Runs on" row is fine.
  • The visual mark is always the K icon in a circle with a green ring, never an emoji or a robot illustration.

Open source: say it exactly like this

The source is public on GitHub; supported self-hosting opens after an independent security review.

Use the sentence as written, anywhere open source comes up: docs, social, pitch. Don't shorten it to "fully open source" or "self-host today", and don't say "coming soon" without the security-review condition. "MIT licensed" can follow in technical contexts.

Before you publish

  • Is every feature mentioned live today?
  • Are button and menu names exactly as the app shows them?
  • Does it use real people or schools without consent? Swap in Demo Junior School instead.
  • Could a parent who has never used KlassApp follow it on a phone?

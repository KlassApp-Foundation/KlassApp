Handoff spec

Isometric projection

2:1 dimetric — every face angle is ±26.57° (slope 0.5). One rule generates the whole scene, so new blocks stay consistent:

  • Top face: (cx−W,cy) (cx,cy−W/2) (cx+W,cy) (cx,cy+W/2)
  • Left face: drops H from the left and bottom vertices
  • Right face: drops H from the bottom and right vertices
  • Stacking: a block resting on another has cy = cy_below − H_self

Blocks: protocol cy 660 · W 250 · H 54, core cy 560 · W 160 · H 100, Toshi cy 468 · W 120 · H 58 (34px float gap above the core). The core is dominant by height, not width — it is the tallest block, while staying narrow enough that the slab keeps a 90px exposed margin for the three protocol cores to sit on. Widen the core and they disappear behind it.

Face shading — flat, no gradients

Depth comes from three flat tones per block, not gradients. That is deliberate: the app's own surfaces carry depth with flat tints and the 1px outline-ring shadow, never gradients, so an illustration full of them would read as foreign inside the app.

Surfacing from inside the cube — the mechanic

The icons belong to the cube, not to a ring around it. Each one grows out of a face centre, holds long enough to be read, then sinks back into the solid — and only one is out at a time per cube, so there is never a crowd to scan. The two visible faces alternate: left, then right, then left.

Core block, 12s linear, three tools at 4s steps. Emergence anchors are the true centroids of the two visible faces — 540,650 (left) and 700,650 (right):

PhaseScaleOpacity
0% — inside the solid0.250
3% — breaks the surface1.061
5% — settles1.001
29% — held for reading1.001
33% — back inside0.250

The 1.06 overshoot is the whole trick: a linear scale-up reads as a tile fading in, while a slight overshoot settling back to 1 reads as something pushed out from within. transform-box: fill-box with transform-origin: 50% 50% makes each tile scale about its own centre — without it, CSS scales SVG groups about the viewBox centre and the tile slides across the illustration as it shrinks.

Emerged tiles must stay inside their face's silhouette — that is what makes them read as sitting on the solid rather than floating beside it. Core tiles clear it easily: a 56px tile on the core's left face spans y 622–678 inside a 614–686 band, and the core block does not bob.

The Toshi tiles are the tight case, because that block bobs. For a 32px tile spanning x 544–576, the face's usable band is bounded by its top edge at the tile's right x (506, not the 489.5 you get from the left x — use the binding corner) and its bottom edge at the tile's left x (548): a 42px band. The -7px bob consumes 7 of that, leaving 35px, so the tiles are 32×32 at local offset y +3.5 (rendered centre 530.5) — about 1.5px of slack at both bob extremes. They previously sat at the centroid (offset 0) at 34×34, which had 3.5px of margin at rest and went 3.5px outside the face at the bob peak. Model tiles carry no ground shadow for the same reason the removed text pill didn't: there is no vertical room for one inside the face.

The real constraint is the slant, not a half-width budget. An earlier version of this note said a tile just had to stay inside the face's 120px half-width — that is wrong and it produced a real bug (a 70×34 pill overhanging the clay face by 11px). The face is a parallelogram of 58px vertical thickness with slope 0.5, so a tile of width w loses w/2 of that thickness to the drop across its own span. The usable band for a tile spanning x 525–595 is only 23px, and the -7px bob eats a further 7, leaving 16px. So: a 34px-tall tile can be at most 48px wide; a 70px-wide tile at most 16px tall. 70×34 does not fit anywhere on this face at any position.

Before shipping any change to a tile's size, re-run the corner test: four corners, evaluated at both b=0 and b=-7, point-in-polygon against that face's quad — M500 468 L620 528 L620 586 L500 526 (left) and M620 528 L740 468 L740 526 L620 586 (right, which mirrors to the same band). Do not substitute a width-only check, do not measure the top edge at the wrong x, and do not test at rest only: every regression on this card so far has been one of those three shortcuts.

Measured: never more than one visible per cube across a full cycle, landing exactly on 540,650 / 700,650 and 560,527 / 680,527. There is a deliberate beat between hand-offs, and it is worth stating exactly because it looks like a bug when you sample it: each cube is empty for ~0.45s between tiles. Derived from the keyframes rather than eyeballed — the core's opacity clears 0.5 from 1.5% to 31% of 12s (3.54s visible, slots every 4s), the models' from 1.17% to 15.35% of 18s (2.55s visible, slots every 3s). Sampling on a 1.2s timer against a 3s slot aliases straight into that gap and will report the cube bare in half your samples; that is the sampler, not the animation. Shorten the gap by extending the hold (12% → 13.5%), never by overlapping two tiles.

The earlier orbit, its dashed track rings and the long routed connector lines are all gone. Do not reinstate them: a ring drawn around the block contradicts the idea that the tools are inside it.

The tool tiles

Upright, never skewed — a tile scales and fades but is never rotated or sheared, so the marks stay square to the viewer at every phase. 56×56, radius 13, --d-white fill, 1px --d-border — same hairline-ring language as .ds-card, sized down from the old static 88×88 because three of them now share the hub's footprint.

Each tile is drawn centred on its own origin (x/y −28) so one translate() keyframe positions it. Draw order puts the tile group after the core block, so a tile on a visible face paints on top of that face — reading as mounted on the solid.

The marks are the real official SVGs from assets/brand/, inlined verbatim — path data untouched, each wrapped in translate(-20 -20) scale() to land at 40px centred on the tile (WhatsApp 0.228291, Drive 0.458191, Slack 0.314964 — the old 44px scales × 40/44, so the path data itself was never retouched). Inlined rather than <image href> so there is no external fetch and the marks are real nodes. They sit on an upright plane precisely so no isometric matrix distorts them — third-party marks must never be skewed, recoloured or monochromed.

Per-tile labels were dropped. A label under a surfacing tile crosses the dark slab and the green face, where --d-text-secondary #64748B fails contrast on both. The marks are self-identifying and the legend beneath names all three — no moving text.

Toshi's icon tile

Toshi carries the real K mark — klassapp-icon.svg, inlined verbatim including its five brand gradients, scaled 0.022 (2000 → 44px).

It sits on a 64px upright tile, radius 14, --d-white fill, 1px --toshi-border #e8e6dc — the border token is Toshi's, not the app's, so the tile belongs to the clay layer it stands on.

Upright, never skewed. The tile's bottom edge lands exactly on the top face's centre (620,468) so it reads as standing on the surface, seated by an iso ellipse rx 27 · ry 13.5 at --toshi-accent-hover (live value #b55738) opacity .16. The rx:ry 2:1 ratio is what makes the shadow lie flat on the plane.

It lives inside the .float-toshi group, so tile, shadow and block bob as one object.

The models, on the Toshi cube

Same mechanic one layer up, and deliberately so — the models belong inside Toshi exactly as the tools belong inside the core. Anchors are the centroids of the Toshi block's two visible faces, 560,527 (left) and 680,527 (right). 18s linear, six marks at 3s steps, one out at a time.

The group sits inside .float-toshi, so the marks bob with the block they live in rather than sliding against it. Nested CSS transforms compose cleanly: the tile's own translate() is in viewBox units and is unaffected by the parent's translateY bob.

The name pill it replaces is gone. Provider names cross-fading in a static pill were a stand-in for marks I did not have. The K tile is untouched — 64px, scale(0.022), still the only thing standing on the top face, and still the one mark that never retracts.

Tiles are 32×32, radius 8, 1px --toshi-border — Toshi's border token, not the app's, so they read as belonging to the clay layer. Marks land in a 22px box; all six are the same size, with no special-cased tile.

Motion

All optional and all inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference).

  • Core tools surfacing: 12s linear infinite, three tiles at 4s steps — out for ~3.5s each, with a 1.06 overshoot on the way out
  • Toshi block: translateY(−7px) over 6s ease-in-out — reinforces that it sits apart from the stack
  • Models surfacing: 18s linear infinite, six marks at 3s steps — out for ~2.55s each, same overshoot, on the Toshi block's own two faces

No bounce, no spring, no scale-up — consistent with the system's restrained motion. Note this illustration adds the reduced-motion guard the product stylesheets still lack.

Blade / CSS structure

Ship as one inline <svg> partial, not an <img> — the marks need to be real nodes and the motion needs CSS reach.

  • resources/views/marketing/partials/layer-tower.blade.php
  • viewBox="216 380 808 518", width:100%; height:auto — scales with no breakpoints
  • Keep role="img" plus the <title>/<desc> pair; the tower carries real meaning
  • Face fills can move to var(--d-…) tokens once the marketing layout links the token sheet
  • Floor hairlines fade out through mask="url(#floorMask)" — a mask, not a visible gradient fill, so the no-gradient rule above still holds. They are symmetric about x=620; never let one terminate outside the slab silhouette.
  • Below ~900px, drop the floor lines; the tiles sit inside the block footprint so nothing needs reflow, and the legend beneath carries all the naming
  • Both animations are pure CSS on <g> elements set to transform-box: fill-box so each tile scales about its own centre; translate() values stay viewBox user units — no JS, no requestAnimationFrame, nothing to tear down on navigation
  • The first frame must never be empty. .orb-a and .mo-1 carry negative delays (-2s / -1.5s) so each cube starts already mid-hold with one tile out. Without them both cubes render bare at t=0 — and t=0 is exactly what a card thumbnail, a PDF export and any screenshot capture. The remaining tiles are re-staggered off those phases (2s/6s; 1.5s…16.5s) so the rhythm and the ~0.4s beat are unchanged. If you retime this, re-check the still frame, not just the loop.
  • Reduced motion: the base rules (.orb-a, .mo-1 at opacity:1) park one tile per cube at full size on a face, so the motionless render is composed rather than empty

Resolved — the six model marks are in

The real official SVGs were supplied and are now inlined from assets/brand/models/<name>-mark.svg: Anthropic, OpenAI, Grok (xAI), Gemini, Kimi (Moonshot) and Z.ai. Nothing was drawn, traced or recoloured — each mark's own path data and fills are untouched, wrapped only in a translate() scale() that centres it on its tile.

They arrived with C2PA provenance metadata and that has to be stripped. Every file carried an <metadata><c2pa:manifest> block of base64 — 8–15 KB each, 54 KB across the seven files supplied, more than the entire rest of this page. Worse, it is fatal when inlined: the xmlns:c2pa namespace is declared on the file's own <svg> root, so lifting the children into this document leaves c2pa:manifest with an unbound prefix and the whole SVG fails to parse. If you re-export these marks, strip the metadata block before inlining — or reference them with <img src>, where the root element survives and the namespace stays bound.

Per-provider scales (each computed from that file's real viewBox, to a 22px box): Anthropic 0.239130 · OpenAI 1.321321 · Grok 0.021484 · Gemini 0.785714 · Kimi 0.88 · Z.ai 0.733333. Gemini and DeepSeek carry their own <defs> gradients, which are preserved — do not flatten them to a solid.

DeepSeek was cut from the set — six slots, not seven. The only artwork supplied for it is a 195.02 × 41.36 wordmark, aspect 4.7:1 against the others' ~1:1. Scaled into a tile it rendered at ~9.8px cap height and read as mush beside six crisp glyphs; a wider tile then failed the face-containment test (width and height trade off on this slant — a 70px-wide tile cannot exceed ~16px in height, so no square-ish wide tile fits), and the 70×14 capsule that did fit turned one slot into a text label among six logos. Each fix traded one defect for another, so the slot is gone rather than shipped broken.

To add it back: DeepSeek's icon-only mark (the whale glyph without the wordmark) drops straight into a standard 32×32 tile at local offset y +3.5, at a scale computed like the other six, and the cycle returns to 21s with 3s steps and delays at −1.5s … 16.5s. Note that seven slots do not alternate faces evenly — an odd count puts two consecutive tiles on the same side once per lap. Ask DeepSeek brand resources for the icon lockup; do not crop the wordmark down to its glyph.

Separate deliverable — hero role rotation

Not part of the tower. Two changes to the hero's rotating role mockup, specified here so both land in the same handoff.

Geometric flip on role change

An X-axis flip — the role panel hinges away top-to-bottom and the next one hinges in behind it, split-flap style. X rather than Y because the hinge axis then echoes the tower's stacked horizontal planes; a Y-axis spin reads as a carousel and fights that.

  • Container: perspective: 900px
  • Panels: transform-style: preserve-3d, backface-visibility: hidden, transform-origin: center
  • Outgoing: rotateX(0) → rotateX(-90deg), opacity 1 → 0, 0.3s ease
  • Incoming: rotateX(90deg) → rotateX(0), opacity 0 → 1, 0.3s ease, delayed 0.15s so the two overlap by half
  • Hold 3.2s per role; if the label wraps to two lines, stagger them 60ms — the same stagger the KPI tiles already use
  • prefers-reduced-motion: reduce → opacity cross-fade at 0.2s, no rotation, no perspective

0.3s ease is --d-transition-slow, the system's existing entrance duration. No bounce, no spring, no overshoot.

K mark as the mockup avatar

Replace the generic avatar placeholder in the rotating chat/phone mockup with the real klassapp-icon.svg. This is Toshi's persistent bot avatar — constant across every message and every tool surface, not a per-sender indicator.

  • Use the real avatar class: .toshi-pill-avatar — 38px, border-radius: 50%, transparent fill, 1px var(--d-green) ring, overflow: hidden, with the mark inside at 24px (.toshi-pill-logo). This already exists in the app CSS and is already Toshi's identity treatment, so the mockup should reuse it rather than introduce a new avatar shape.
  • The circle is safe — don't substitute a square. Measured, the mark's ink reaches only 0.909 of its half-width from centre, so at 24px inside a 38px circle it clears the clip by 8.1px, and by 1.7px even drawn edge-to-edge. There is no cropping problem to design around.
  • Set box-sizing: border-box. The real class declares width: 38px plus a 1px border; it renders 38px total in the app because Tailwind's preflight supplies a global border-box reset. This design-system bundle does not ship Tailwind, so in a bare page the same declaration renders 40px.
  • --d-white fill is not used — the real class is transparent, so the bubble or pill colour shows through the ring. Keep it that way.
  • Inline the SVG rather than <img>, and strip the C2PA <metadata> block: its xmlns:c2pa is declared on the root, so inlining the children alone leaves an unbound prefix and the whole SVG fails to parse. This bit me building the tower.

## 1. Parties and purpose — draft for legal review

The School uses KlassApp to manage school records and to produce and share report cards. This agreement sets out what data KlassApp processes for the School, why, who can see it, how long it is kept, and what each party must do.

The School is the body that decides what learner and family data is collected and used. KlassApp processes that data only on the School's instructions, as set out in this agreement and the product documentation.

## 2. Data processed — draft for legal review

KlassApp processes the following categories for the School:

- **Learner records:** name, identifier, class, date of birth, guardianship links, and the school's own notes about enrolment.
- **Parent and guardian records:** name, contact details, and the link to each learner.
- **Marks and assessment records:** marks, grades, aggregates, divisions, positions, comments, and released report cards.
- **Attendance records:** presence, absence, lateness, session dates and any comment the school records.
- **Account and access records:** sign-in identifiers, roles, and an audit log of who changed what.

The School decides which optional fields it collects. KlassApp does not add data categories on its own.

## 3. Roles — draft for legal review

- **The School decides.** It decides the purpose of processing, which learners and families are recorded, who may see which records, when a report is released, and when data is deleted.
- **KlassApp processes.** It hosts, stores, backs up and displays the data, produces report cards and messages the School asks it to send, and follows the School's written instructions.
- **KlassApp does not sell the data**, use it for advertising, or use one school's data to serve another school.

## 4. Consent and parent notice — draft for legal review

The School is responsible for giving parents and guardians notice of what is recorded and why, and for obtaining any consent the law requires. KlassApp provides the School with the wording it uses in the product; the School decides whether that wording is adequate for its own jurisdiction and adds anything its law requires.

Where a parent or learner must agree to something before a report is shared, the School obtains that agreement. KlassApp records the agreement where the product offers the field, and does not treat silence as consent.

## 5. Who can see what — draft for legal review

Access follows the role the School gives a person:

- **School administrators and head teachers** see the school's records, within that school only.
- **Class teachers** see the classes they head; **subject teachers** see the subjects and classes they teach.
- **Parents and learners** see only their own child or their own records, and only after the School releases the report.
- **KlassApp staff** access records only to operate, support or secure the service, under a logged support process, and only when the School asks or when there is a security incident.

A person from another school cannot see this School's records. The product checks the school on every request.

## 6. Retention and deletion — draft for legal review

- Records are kept for as long as the School's account is active and the School needs them.
- On a written request, KlassApp deletes or returns the School's records within an agreed period, except where a backup still holds a copy for a stated, limited period.
- A learner or parent record deleted by the School is removed from the active system; backups rotate out on their normal cycle.
- KlassApp does not keep a separate copy of school data for its own purposes.

The precise retention periods and the backup rotation are completed in the schedule to this agreement.

## 7. Security — draft for legal review

KlassApp will:

- encrypt data in transit and at rest where the hosting service supports it;
- keep access role-based and logged;
- keep secrets in a managed secret store, not in the source code;
- apply security updates to the application and its dependencies;
- test access rules, including cross-school refusals, before a release;
- keep a verified backup before a schema change and test the restore.

No system is perfectly secure. KlassApp does not promise that a breach will never happen; it promises the controls above and the notice in clause 9.

## 8. Sub-processors — draft for legal review

KlassApp uses service providers for hosting, database, file storage, email, messaging and monitoring. A list is kept in the schedule and updated when a provider changes. KlassApp remains responsible to the School for what a sub-processor does with the data.

A new sub-processor that processes learner or parent data is notified to the School before it is used, and the School may object on reasonable grounds.

## 9. Breach notice — draft for legal review

If KlassApp becomes aware of a personal-data breach affecting the School's records, it will notify the School without undue delay, and in any event within the period stated in the schedule. The notice will say what happened, which records and how many are affected, what KlassApp has done, and what the School should do.

The School is responsible for notifying parents, regulators or others where its law requires it. KlassApp will provide the information the School reasonably needs for that notice.

## 10. Assistance and audit — draft for legal review

KlassApp will help the School answer a parent or learner request about access, correction or deletion, and will answer reasonable questions about how the service meets this agreement. An audit that requires access to another school's data is not allowed; a security questionnaire or a report from an independent test is the usual route.

## 11. Term, termination and return — draft for legal review

This agreement runs while the School uses KlassApp. On termination, the School may export its records in a usable format for an agreed period, after which KlassApp deletes them subject to the backup rotation. Fees and notice are set in the commercial agreement, not here.

## 12. Changes — draft for legal review

A change to this agreement is agreed in writing. A change that adds a data category, a new sub-processor or a new purpose needs the School's agreement before it takes effect.
